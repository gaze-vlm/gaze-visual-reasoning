Drawing Strategies During Human-Robot Symbolic Communication

Dario Valentini1,2, Matteo Moro1, Vittorio Murino2,3, Lucia Schiatti1,2,4
2DIBRIS, University of Genoa, 2AIGO, Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia, 2University of Verona, 2CSAIL, Massachusetts Institute of Technology

Abstract

Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have achieved remarkable progress in visual recognition and processing, yet they struggle with high-level reasoning about complex scenes. Human gaze provides a task-dependent top-down signal of visual attention that reflects human ability to recognize entities and infer relationships and events. To investigate whether such behavioral cues can improve reasoning in open-source VLMs, we extended CogBench, a benchmark for structured visual reasoning, with eye-tracking data collected from human participants performing recognition and reasoning tasks. We evaluate two gaze integration strategies with varying levels of learned adaptation on five LLaVA-based models. Our results show that the effectiveness of gaze integration is model-dependent and primarily benefits specific reasoning dimensions rather than mere visual recognition. Overall, our findings motivate further controlled investigation of human gaze as a behavioral prior for high-level visual reasoning.
